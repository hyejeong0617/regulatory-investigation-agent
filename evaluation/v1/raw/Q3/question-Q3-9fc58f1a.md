Direct answer
- For dried figs themselves, the supplied table does not impose a different limit merely because they are destined for sorting: the general sorting category for dried fruits expressly excludes products listed under 1.1.3, which is the dried-fig entry. (Regulation 2023/915, ANNEX I / table 1 / HTML row 4 / entry 1.1.1 [E1b9753e43915923f]; Regulation 2023/915, ANNEX I / table 1 / HTML row 6 / entry 1.1.3 [E0c17d35a62395acf])
  Condition: This conclusion concerns dried figs classified under entry 1.1.3, rather than a separate processed product.

Legal requirements
- The supplied Annex I entry lists maximum levels for dried figs of 6.0 μg/kg for aflatoxin B1 and 10.0 μg/kg for the sum of B1, B2, G1 and G2. (Regulation 2023/915, ANNEX I / table 1 / HTML row 6 / entry 1.1.3 [E0c17d35a62395acf])
  Condition: The table headers identify the two numeric columns as B1 and the sum of B1, B2, G1 and G2.
- For a processed product consisting at least 80% of dried figs, the dried-fig maximum levels also apply; for other cases, Article 3(1) and (2) apply. (Regulation 2023/915, ANNEX I / table 1 / HTML row 6 / entry 1.1.3 [E0c17d35a62395acf])
  Condition: This rule concerns a processed product, not merely dried figs intended for later processing.
- Where Article 3 applies because no specific Union maximum level is set for the relevant processed food, the factors to take into account include concentration changes from drying or dilution and processing, ingredient proportions, and the analytical limit of quantification. (Regulation 2023/915, Article 3 [E430e8b5474e74155])
  Condition: Article 3(1) applies where no specific Union maximum level is set out for the dried, diluted, processed or compound food.
